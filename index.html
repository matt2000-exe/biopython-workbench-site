Tools like SnapGene and Geneious are excellent — and expensive, and online-only, or both. Biopython Workbench covers the everyday 80%: quick sequence checks, restriction digests, primer sanity checks, light cloning work, and a rough tree, entirely offline, for free, with nothing to configure.

What it does

Import once, then explore every sequence through the same set of tools.

Batch import & analysis

FASTA/GenBank files or pasted text, many sequences at once. A bad file in the batch doesn't take down the rest — failures are isolated per-sequence.

Per-sequence stats

Nucleotide counts, GC%, molecular weight, melting temp (Wallace), 6-frame ORF finder, translation, codon usage split into Start/Stop/Other groups, GC skew, reverse complement, and start/stop codon highlighting with a live count in the row label.

Restriction sites & primers

Customizable enzyme and common-primer panels (T7/T3/SP6/M13 etc.), each with "Reset to Default" — a summary list plus an annotated on-sequence map, with an explicit "None found" rather than a blank sequence, and a prompt to re-run automatically when your selection changes.

Visualizations

A circular plasmid/vector map with annotations, restriction sites, and primers, plus a GC-content sliding-window graph — copyable and savable as images from the results view, or via right-click "Copy Image" on the row itself.

Pairwise comparison

Global alignment with identity/mismatch/gap stats and a classic 3-line alignment view.

Phylogenetic tree

Builds its own progressive multiple-sequence alignment and clusters it via neighbor-joining — entirely offline, no external aligner. A practical approximation, clearly labeled as such, with guidance toward dedicated tools for publication-grade trees. Tree image is copyable and savable too.

In-silico digest & virtual gel

A fragment-size table plus a virtual gel chart: log-scale bands, largest fragments near the top, just like a real agarose gel photo.

Primer design tool

Paste any primer and get Tm, GC%, and rough self-dimer/hairpin risk heuristics.

Sequence Builder

Hand-edit a sequence in a pop-out window — splice a gene into a vector backbone, for example. Verify checks it's a real DNA/RNA/protein sequence, toggle Highlight Codons / Highlight Promoters, run the full analysis pipeline inline, then save it back into your results. Add Insert locates a known promoter (T7/SP6/T3), splices in a validated coding insert right after it, and highlights exactly what changed.

Who it's for

  • Students learning molecular biology who need quick, understandable answers.
  • Educators demonstrating sequence analysis concepts without licensing headaches.
  • Biotech hobbyists & citizen scientists working on their own projects.
  • Molecular biology researchers doing routine cloning and sequence-checking who want something fast, free, and local.

Sample data

Don't have a sequence handy? These are real, public records pulled directly from NCBI — not made up, not tracked, just files you can open in the app and click around in.

sample-sequences.fasta

A multi-FASTA file with five real NCBI records: the pUC19 cloning vector (M77789.2), the original jellyfish GFP gene (M62653.1) and its protein (AAA27721.1), and two Hepatitis E virus genomes (PQ160068.1, JQ013791.1). Good for batch import, stats, ORF finding, translation, and the phylogenetic tree.

Download .fasta

sample-pBR322-plasmid.gb

The classic pBR322 cloning vector (J01749.1, 4,361 bp, circular) as a fully annotated GenBank record — ampicillin (bla) and tetracycline (tet) resistance genes, the ROP protein, and an origin of replication. Good for the plasmid map, restriction digest, and GenBank annotation display.
