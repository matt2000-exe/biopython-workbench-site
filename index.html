Tools like SnapGene and Geneious are excellent — and expensive, and online-only, or both. Biopython Workbench covers the everyday 80%: quick sequence checks, restriction digests, primer sanity checks, and a rough tree, entirely offline, for free, with nothing to configure.

What it does

Import once, then explore every sequence through the same set of tools.

Batch import & analysis

FASTA/GenBank files or pasted text, many sequences at once. A bad file in the batch doesn't take down the rest — failures are isolated per-sequence.

Per-sequence stats

Nucleotide counts, GC%, molecular weight, melting temp (Wallace), 6-frame ORF finder, translation, codon usage table, GC skew, reverse complement, and start/stop codon highlighting.

Restriction sites & primers

Customizable enzyme panel and a common-primer panel (T7/T3/SP6/M13 etc.), each with its own selection dialog, "Reset to Default," and a prompt to refresh results when your selection changes.

Visualizations

A circular plasmid/vector map with annotations, restriction sites, and primers, plus a GC-content sliding-window graph — both copyable and savable as images directly from the results view.

Pairwise comparison

Global alignment with identity/mismatch/gap stats and a classic 3-line alignment view.

Phylogenetic tree

Builds its own progressive multiple-sequence alignment and clusters it via neighbor-joining — entirely offline, no external aligner. A practical approximation, clearly labeled as such, with guidance toward dedicated tools for publication-grade trees.

In-silico digest & virtual gel

A fragment-size table plus a virtual gel chart: log-scale bands, largest fragments near the top, just like a real agarose gel photo.

Primer design tool

Paste any primer and get Tm, GC%, and rough self-dimer/hairpin risk heuristics.

Sequence search

Find and highlight every occurrence of a typed fragment in a sequence, with a live match count.

Split view

Two independent result panes, each with its own search box, for eyeballing two sequences side by side.

Sessions

Save and load a full analysis session as a JSON project file, so you can pick up right where you left off.

Export

Analysis report as .txt, sequences as multi-FASTA — and it reveals the saved file in File Explorer when it's done.

Who it's for

  • Students learning molecular biology who need quick, understandable answers.
  • Educators demonstrating sequence analysis concepts without licensing headaches.
  • Biotech hobbyists & citizen scientists working on their own projects.
  • Molecular biology researchers doing routine cloning and sequence-checking who want something fast, free, and local.
